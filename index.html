Evidence at a glance

What the estimates support

The strongest replicated result concerns commercial scale. Creative classifications are more uncertain, and the market models remain exploratory.

01

Capacity is the clearest production correlate

The log–log coefficient is 1.007 with a 95% HC3 interval of 0.530–1.485 and p < 0.001. A 1% capacity difference is associated with about a 1.01% gross difference, conditional on included controls.

02

Adaptation and revival evidence is weaker

Adaptation is positive (0.287) and revival negative (−0.284), but neither clears the 5% threshold. These are associations, not causal effects.

03

Market inference has a narrower evidence base

Unemployment is absent for 244 of 501 weeks. The missing pattern is concentrated in earlier years, so complete-case estimates cannot represent the full market series.

Methods and evidence boundary

Designed to be auditable

The repository separates exact aggregate-level reproduction from new exploratory analysis and from claims the supplied data cannot support.

01

Two units of analysis

Production aggregates answer a cross-sectional question; weekly market totals answer a time-series question. Their coefficients are never compared as if they share an observation unit.

02

Robust uncertainty

HC3 protects the small production sample against leverage-sensitive heteroskedasticity. HAC with eight lags addresses short-run serial correlation in the exploratory weekly models.

03

Deterministic checks

Shape, missingness, transformations, benchmarks, sample definitions, figures, and site artifacts are validated by automated tests.

04

Restricted source boundary

The original workbook and row-level observations remain private pending redistribution permission. Public artifacts contain estimates and derived summaries only.

Known limitations

What this study cannot identify

  • No causal effect of adding seats: high-demand productions may be assigned larger theatres.
  • No reconstruction of the unavailable 4,972 show-week microdata or full selection trail.
  • No verified controls for budget, star casting, marketing, reviews, or ticket pricing.
  • Market matching is incomplete, repeated-frequency variables are coarse, and the pandemic is a structural break.
